📐 How this upgraded model calculates things

Nonlinear battery: Open-circuit voltage is interpolated from a hand-picked Li-ion-like voltage/state-of-charge table. Charge is updated by coulomb counting. Internal resistance rises as charge falls and varies with temperature. This is illustrative, not fitted to a cell manufacturer's data.

Electrical losses: Battery and wiring dissipate I²R. Capacitor current is (Vcap − Vbus) / ESR, and its heat is Icap² × ESR. Voltage on the capacitor bank changes as dV/dt = −Icap / C.

Power conversion: Load wattage is measured at a regulated 3.3 V output. The battery/capacitor must supply Pout / efficiency at the input. Efficiency declines a little at light loads. The solver looks for the stable higher-voltage operating point.

Protection: The battery has a discharge-current ceiling and the converter has an input-current ceiling. If requested power cannot be maintained above the input undervoltage threshold, or the converter would need more current than allowed, output is shut off. It retries after a one-second pause with 0.25 V hysteresis. These are intentionally simplified controller behaviors, not real protection circuitry.

Heating: Battery, capacitor, and converter each use a lumped thermal mass and Newton-style cooling dT/dt = (heat − cooling×(T−ambient)) / thermal mass. Different materials, thermal interfaces, cooling geometry, entropic heat and thermal runaway are not simulated.

Numerics: A small time step integrates two independent systems side by side. The capacitor is initially charged to the battery's open-circuit voltage. Loss power is integrated through time; graphs sample the model for plotting.

Do not use this for real battery-pack sizing or protection design. It includes all the missing effects named in Bench 01, but every one is a simplified mathematical approximation, not a tested component model. It does not cover datasheet current-duration curves, cycle aging, cell mismatch, inductance/transients, capacitor voltage rating, thermal runaway, fault analysis, or certified protection requirements.
